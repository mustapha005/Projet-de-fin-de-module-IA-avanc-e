# Limited compute sharpens breast ultrasound comparisons

SAN-1 can make a useful contribution by measuring how adaptation strategy and training-label availability interact under a declared CPU budget, then checking the resulting ordering on an external breast-ultrasound cohort. Medical DINOv2 adaptation comparisons, low-label LoRA studies, and cross-dataset ultrasound evaluations already exist. The proposed study therefore needs a precise account of its constraints and a reproducible comparison, with no claim to be the first use of these methods in medicine or the first BUSI-to-BrEaST evaluation. Recent evidence also makes data provenance and pretraining exposure central to interpreting external performance. The defensible outcome is a conditional answer about the tested backbone, cohorts, label allocation, and compute ceiling; no experimental answer is available yet. This positioning follows the closest verified studies, rather than an exhaustive novelty claim. ([Baharoon et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.02366v4), [Lian et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.12215v1), [Wang](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4418/16/10/1537))

## Existing adaptation studies define the comparison

DINOv2 supplies a clear rationale for a serious frozen-feature baseline: its published evaluation includes transfer with precomputed representations. Its natural-image evidence does not establish how those representations behave on breast ultrasound. LoRA supplies a parameter-efficient adaptation mechanism, but the original language-model experiments do not establish proportional CPU savings for a vision transformer. **Trainable parameter count and measured computation are separate outcomes.** SAN-1 should preserve that distinction when interpreting a small adapter that still requires computation through the backbone. ([Oquab et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07193v2), [Hu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685))

Baharoon and colleagues already compare linear probing, full fine-tuning, LoRA and BitFit on DINOv2 radiology benchmarks. Their large backbone and adaptation settings differ from the proposed ViT-S/14 study. Lian and colleagues investigate medical-model LoRA at the same nominal training-label fractions proposed for SAN-1, include rank ablations, and examine DINOv2 in an additional comparison. Dutt and colleagues provide a broader medical PEFT benchmark. Together, these papers locate the contribution in the joint restriction of hardware, adaptation strategy, labels and external evaluation. Their scores cannot be imported as matched baselines because the modalities, datasets, backbones and training procedures differ. ([Baharoon et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.02366v4), [Lian et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.12215v1), [Dutt et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v250/dutt24a.html))

The ordering of the methods remains an empirical question. Feature-distortion research explains why fine-tuning can improve internal performance while underperforming a frozen representation under distribution shift. Partial-tuning research identifies layer choice as a consequential design variable. These findings justify measuring internal and external rankings separately and comparing adaptation locations. They do not establish that the final two DINOv2 blocks, a particular LoRA rank, or a frozen classifier will be optimal on BUSI. ([Kumar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10054), [Ye et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15681))

## Ultrasound research makes pretraining exposure consequential

The closest recent ultrasound benchmarks narrow the novelty claim further. Ramollari and colleagues report a multi-seed cross-dataset comparison that includes DINOv2, with a configuration-specific failure of the full-frame model and a separate exploratory lesion-crop analysis. This is evidence to investigate representation and image-framing failures, not a prediction of SAN-1's results. Wang evaluates several ultrasound cohorts, including BUSI and BrEaST, and considers external calibration and region-of-interest inputs. An identifier-based split in that study does not independently prove that the public BUSI images have reliable patient identities. ([Ramollari et al.](https://www.mdpi.com/1999-4893/19/11/1011), [Wang](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4418/16/10/1537))

Modality-specific models provide important context without resolving the proposed comparison. USFM studies label-efficient ultrasound transfer; BUSGen uses generative pretraining and downstream data generation; TinyUSFM studies compact models through distillation. These approaches change pretraining data, supervision or model construction, so their published gains do not answer which adaptation of the fixed DINOv2 backbone is useful within the project's CPU ceiling. Their availability does, however, require explaining why a general-purpose backbone was selected and how a later modality-specific extension would be evaluated. ([Jiao et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38788326/), [Yu et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-026-01639-1), [Ma et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19239v2))

**A modality-specific replacement can compromise an external holdout through pretraining exposure.** USF-MAE's OpenUS-46 table includes BrEaST. Substituting that model would require an explicit exposure audit and a qualified interpretation of the test cohort. The same audit principle applies to any backbone; a general-purpose model's name alone is not proof that its entire pretraining corpus is disjoint from every evaluation image. SAN-1 should record what is known and what cannot be established about exposure, rather than equating “not fine-tuned on BrEaST” with complete unseen-data provenance. ([Megahed et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22990v2))

## Dataset provenance limits the meaning of center shift

BUSI provides the intended development cohort, but the original descriptor is not sufficient evidence of an independent patient-level partition. The subsequent critique identifies geometrically transformed duplicates, similar lesion frames and conflicting labels, and supplies supplementary grouping information. A zero-crossing check at the specified pHash threshold establishes only that narrowly defined similarity condition. Before freeze, compare the prepared partitions against the published grouping evidence and record any unresolved relationships; do not label an image split as a patient split without a defensible mapping. ([BUSI descriptor](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6906728/), [Pawłowska et al.](https://www.ippt.pan.pl/repository/open/o8361.pdf))

This distinction is consistent with CLAIM's recommendation to report partition disjointness at the appropriate level. A recent kidney-CT study also distinguishes recovered acquisition groups from patient identities. Its effect sizes and grouping method are not transferable ultrasound results, but the conceptual warning applies: image-similarity screening cannot silently substitute for verified patient independence. The remaining uncertainty should constrain the internal-test claims and the bootstrap resampling unit. ([CLAIM 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11304031/), [Uçar](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42955168/))

BrEaST is an external acquisition cohort with public case-level labels. Its descriptor reports 154 benign cases, 98 cancers and four normal cases, with biopsy or follow-up verification. The normal cases must be excluded for the planned binary task. Medical-center linkage was removed during de-identification, which limits center-stratified analysis of the public release. Consequently, BUSI-to-BrEaST performance measures a combined cohort/domain change involving acquisition practice, case mix and reference standards. It does not isolate a causal center effect, and the cohort should not be described as entirely biopsy-confirmed. These source counts still require reconciliation with the actual local inventory. ([BrEaST descriptor](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-024-02984-z))

## The protocol must separate detectability, calibration and cost

The “not worse” clause in H1 needs careful treatment. Failure to detect a superiority difference does not establish equivalence or noninferiority. Under the supplied detectability rule, a comparison whose interval includes zero should remain inconclusive for that clause unless a justified noninferiority margin and corresponding procedure are specified before results. The literature reviewed here supplies no validated clinical margin for this particular AUC comparison. H2 also needs explicit clause logic: rank preservation and a parameter-count relationship with the generalization gap are separate propositions. ([Lakens](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5502906/))

Seed variability and uncertainty from test sampling answer different questions. Repeated predictions on the same test images must not be treated as independent patients. The protocol should state the pairing and resampling unit, how seed-specific differences are aggregated, how resamples with only one class are handled, and how the family of planned contrasts is interpreted. These are proposed implementation requirements, not an already validated bootstrap procedure for SAN-1. CLAIM provides the broader reporting basis for making selection rules, data structure and computational conditions explicit. ([CLAIM 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11304031/))

External discrimination and calibration should remain separate outputs. The original calibration work distinguishes confidence from empirical correctness, while recent mammography and retinal studies demonstrate why transfer assessment benefits from domain-specific discrimination and calibration reporting. Their modalities, heads and calibration procedures differ from SAN-1. A model chosen by validation AUC is therefore not automatically the best-calibrated external model, and any post-hoc calibration added later must be fitted using source validation data under a preregistered rule. ([Guo et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html), [Nguyen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10358), [Poyrazer et al.](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2026.1815982/full))

For compute, an equal ceiling does not imply equal consumption. The protocol should charge and expose feature extraction, tuning and final training separately, including the one-time and amortized costs of the LP cache. It must also settle LP's feature-view policy: a cache does not reproduce fresh per-epoch augmentation by itself. Likewise, the label fractions describe training labels; the unchanged validation cohort contributes additional labeled examples. These are methodological deductions from the proposed design, not findings from the cited studies. Detailed hardware, software, frozen-parameter and selection reporting follows established medical-imaging reporting guidance; the broader FM review supplies context rather than a method-ranking prediction. ([CLAIM 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11304031/), [Niu et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42616634/))

## What the completed study could establish

A useful final result would state the circumstances under which a method earns its extra adaptation cost, or show that the measured differences remain inconclusive. An honest conditional result requires preserving the distinction between a reproducible image-level benchmark and evidence of patient-level or clinical generalization. If the known BUSI relationships cannot be resolved, that limitation should determine the strength of the internal evidence before any attractive AUC is observed.
